Dalecrest roadmap

This document is the one plan for moving Dalecrest from a small simulated village to one people want to watch, follow and take part in. It describes work to be done. Nothing here is implemented or deployed by being written down.

  • The accepted architecture is the resident knowledge graph. Where this plan and that document differ, the knowledge graph governs and this plan is wrong.
  • Design decisions describes the village as it runs today.
  • Give Dalecrest residents something worth following is design background. It argued for the promise pilot that is now the first milestone, and its reasoning is worth reading. Its stage list is superseded by this one.

§ 3Review: how the residents think

Stage numbers refer to the plan below. Each finding is marked by whether the fault it describes has since been mended.

  1. Clock and queue. Stage 0 made the village clock independent of model latency. A resident can wait while others continue.
  2. Routine decisions. Stage 0 lets a resident continue sleeping under a valid earlier instruction. The activity log records the original model choice.
  3. The action model is shown too little. actionBody in decisions.mjs sends needs, stores, two memories and a menu. It leaves out relationships, open obligations and what the resident has learned. (Stages 1 and 2)
    The request carries the resident's goals as they now stand, recalled memories with their dates, open requests with the warmth and trust between the two, the household, their own life, cold and the weather.
  4. Place names. Stage 0 added readable place names to option descriptions. Choice IDs still identify the exact action.
  5. Moot attendance. A scripted test exercises quorum and construction on the model-driven path. How often real residents choose to attend remains a pilot measurement. Standing-instruction tests
    The test is in. The live measurement is not.
  6. Memory is a short list of sentences. remember in engine/civic.mjs keeps 18 strings with no source, importance or recall. (Stages 1 and 2)
    Memories carry a source, importance and place, a resident keeps sixty, recall is scored, and every memory is stored in PostgreSQL.
  7. Everyone knows everything. The menu and the request use the true stores and every resident's true destination. “Bake” disappears from a menu the moment someone far away uses the last wood. (Stage 1)
    Flour and firewood are known only from an oven inspection or from being told. Barley and water are seen on depositing. Other knowledge is still shared by all.
  8. Residents cannot ask, agree, decline or vote. Encounters pick one of five templates. The original moot assigned votes. One template is wrong in effect: “discussed the dwindling grain reserve” fires when a store is under a quarter of its capacity, so building a granary makes it fire more often. (Stage 1 for asking and agreeing, Stage 3 for the fix, Stage 4 for votes)
    Asking, agreeing and declining are model choices, and so are asking to marry and answering. The grain sentence now fires on food counted in days. Each attendee now chooses an explicit ballot.
  9. Relationships are one number that only rises. After 60 days, 19 of 122 are at the maximum. (Stages 2 and 3)
    Warmth and trust are separate, both fall, and warmth fades and fills more slowly near the top.
  10. People only arrive, and the economy has no pressure. Nobody is born, grows old, falls ill or leaves. One harvest shift feeds the founding village for a day. (Stage 3)
    The harvest is seasonal and a winter has to be stored for, and residents are born, fall ill, marry and leave.

What is already right and should be kept: the simulation is deterministic and replays from JSON; every model exchange is recorded before it is applied; the engine checks a chosen action again before starting it; and the map is data the village owns.

§ 4Review: how it looks

The map and the walking view are sound and draw the same data. What is missing is legibility and life.

  • Nothing marks that something is happening. A request, a moot and a building site look like an empty lane.
    A resident who is deciding carries a question mark on the map and rests a hand on the chin in the walking view, and a site shows as stakes, then a frame. A note marks where each recent chronicle entry happened, in both views, and the director takes a watcher there. A request or a moot in progress still has no mark of its own.
  • Every trade is the same arm movement, and a resting resident stands outside their door.
    Both views draw the tools and motions of the work. Smoke and lit windows show where a hearth is in use.
  • A selected resident is not in the URL, a fresh page always starts with Agnes, and only the latest 100 chronicle entries can be reached.
    The selected resident is in the address, so a link or a refresh keeps them. A bare address now opens on the director’s watch. The chronicle still stops at 100.
  • There is no season, weather, sound or change with the hour on the map.
    Both views show the weather and the hour. The Sound toggle brings in the river, weather, animals and nearby work.
  • The walking view can only follow. It rebuilds the whole scene whenever the map changes.
    A visitor can look around, follow a resident, or hold on a chronicle entry. Map revisions update the affected scenery while the camera and residents stay in place.
  • The page fetches every resident's whole record every 2.5 seconds.
    The server streams a snapshot when a viewer connects and sends changed fields and entries after that.

§ 5Principles

  1. The clock does not wait for a model. Village time advances on its own schedule. Budget and latency limit how fresh a resident's decisions are, never how fast time passes.
  2. Models decide. The engine establishes what is true. A goal, a choice and a ballot come from a model. Whether the action could start, what it changed and who saw it come from the engine.
  3. Ask a model when there is a choice to make. Routine follows an instruction a model already gave. A real tradeoff gets a fresh decision.
  4. Keep three kinds of knowledge apart. What the world holds, what each resident knows and what the chronicle can verify are different records, and the plan never lets one stand in for another.
  5. Record how each activity came about. Fresh choice, standing instruction or a wait. A viewer and an operator can both tell which.
  6. Pressure before complexity. A mind needs something to weigh. Add one real need and prove a story on it before adding systems.
  7. Everything notable has an address. A resident, a promise, an event and a day can each be linked to and returned to.

The clock advances independently. A routine follows a valid standing instruction from an earlier model choice. When no instruction fits, the resident waits for a fresh decision. The thresholds remain pilot parameters.

  • A clock that advances independently. Action requests run alongside the village clock. In OpenAI mode the default pace is 30 real seconds per village minute, or two village days per real day. The pilot will measure how that pace feels to viewers.
    Commit 19c3f47. Tested by “village time passes when no decision ever arrives”.
  • Standing instructions. A standing instruction is a past model choice that the engine may repeat while the situation it answered still holds. It records:
    • the routine it covers, such as sleeping at night, a morning meal or the next shift at the resident's trade
    • the action chosen, and the exchange that chose it
    • the goal versions and open commitments in force when it was chosen
    • when it lapses
    An instruction keeps its routine, action, exchange, goals and lapse. Anything touching a request for help is always a fresh choice, so no commitment is stored on it.
  • When a fresh decision is needed. The engine requests one when any of these holds at an activity boundary:
    • no standing instruction covers the situation
    • the instruction has lapsed, or the goals or commitments it was chosen under have changed
    • the resident has learned something consequential since, such as a request, a shortage seen or a promise broken
    • following the instruction would cost the resident something they have a stake in, such as missing a commitment that falls due
    • two needs are urgent at once
    • the instructed action can no longer be started
    The engine checks time needed for an accepted work request and an approaching moot. Personal appointments such as the breakfast example remain a design proposal.

Breakfast shows the difference. Alice's morning meal is routine and follows her standing instruction. On the morning she has promised to meet Edith at the well, the engine can see that eating first would run past the hour she promised. That is a tradeoff, so the same breakfast is put to the Decisions API as a choice, with the promise in view.

  • What happens when things go wrong.

The promise pilot

Size L · First milestone In hand

Build one promise that can survive a night's sleep, and let a viewer find out how it ended.

Implemented so far Residents can check the communal oven, share dated observations, ask a neighbour for supplies and choose how to answer. An accepted request offers a specific shift the resident can choose to keep their word. A requester who learns of a later count or delivery can keep the request open, withdraw it in person or decide later. An ordinary shift changes the stores and earns its worker credit for that work alone. Trace a request and its reply · Trace a reconsideration

Mabel Ash needs wood for the oven. Oswin Holt cuts it. Mabel may ask Oswin for help, and he may accept, decline or leave it unanswered. An accepted promise can be kept, broken or released by Mabel. Each ending has a record a viewer can return to find.

RecordWhat it holdsIn the pilot

What gets built

  • Outcomes. For oven and woodcutting shifts, record the site, the participants, the actual change to the stores and any commitment the choice named, in the same transaction as the change. Record a fruitless or interrupted shift too.
    Commit b6d7b8d, with work-outcomes.test.mjs.

    All seven statuses exist in engine/exchanges.mjs. A request asks for one shift's output within 24 village hours.

Goals and memory

Size M · Needs the pilot's observations and commitments Built
Reading reviews

Give each resident goals that persist and change for reasons, set by the general-purpose model.

  • Two kinds of goal.
    Each goal is a version with an id, kept in actor.goalBook by engine/goals.mjs. A goal with a condition names one from a fixed vocabulary: a store level, a place reached, a number of finished shifts, each within so many hours, or an open request kept. The engine settles it met, missed or overtaken each minute and records why. A goal on evidence has no condition and may name the resident it concerns. The founding goals became goals on evidence.

Example“Four buckets in the barrels by dusk.”“Earn Edith's trust.”

  • Goal reviews. A review is triggered when a resident needs direction, when a goal is met or missed, or when they learn something consequential. Between reviews the last valid goals are reused, including when the goal-setting model is unavailable. Reviews have their own spending cap so they never take turns from action choices. A review may also set or renew standing instructions for the resident's routines.
    A review is called when a goal is settled, when a resident learns something of importance 8 or more, or at seven in the morning after three days without one. Claude Haiku 5.5 reviews one resident at a time, from goal-reviews.mjs, under its own cap of 30,000 tokens a rolling hour. The engine checks each decision before applying it, and a goal about another resident changes only on cited evidence. Between reviews, and without a key, residents keep the goals they have. A changed goal ends the standing instructions chosen under the old one. Reviews do not yet set standing instructions.
  • The chain in PostgreSQL. Each link is stored, so an operator can follow a resident from a source event to an outcome without replaying the village.
    1. An event in the world becomes an observation for each resident who saw or heard of it, with source and time.
    2. A goal review reads observations and returns goal versions. Each version records its owner, its kind, its condition if it has one, the observations it rests on and the exchange that produced it.
    3. An action exchange records the goal versions, commitments and observations it was given, the menu and the choice.
    4. The outcome is linked to that choice.
    5. The outcome becomes an observation for whoever witnessed it, and may trigger the next review.
    dalecrest.observations holds every memory, linked to its chronicle entry. dalecrest.goal_reviews holds each exchange as sent and received. dalecrest.goal_versions holds every version with the observations and evidence it rests on and the review that made it. Each action trace records the goal versions and memories its request was given. The view dalecrest.goal_chain reads the chain from an observation to the outcome and its witness in one query.
  • Memory. Replace the 18-sentence list with stored observations that carry source, time, the people and place involved and an importance. For any request, recall by recency, importance and relevance to the people and place at hand. Start with scored SQL.
    Memories carry an id, an importance, a source, the place and the people involved. A resident keeps sixty and forgets the least memorable first. recall scores them by recency, importance and relevance to the people and place at hand, and the action request and goal reviews are given what it recalls. Every memory is stored in PostgreSQL. Recall is scored in the engine, not yet in SQL.
  • Relationships with substance. Separate warmth from trust. Mabel can stay fond of Oswin and become less willing to rely on him after a missed promise. Both move on recorded events and both can fall.
    Kept apart in engine/bonds.mjs. Warmth rises with company, rises less between people already close, and fades between residents who stop meeting. Trust grows a little with acquaintance and otherwise moves on requests as each resident learns of them. Each move is kept as evidence about that person.
  • A resident's own words. A review may return one sentence in the resident's voice explaining a revision. It is published as the resident's stated intention. It is not presented as a verified motive.
    A review may return one sentence in the resident's voice about its most important change. A sentence that is not one plain sentence, or that carries a link or markup, is not published. One that passes is recorded as an "In their words" chronicle entry, worded as what the resident said.

Done when a verifiable goal is settled by the engine with the outcome that settled it; a relational goal is kept, revised or retired by a review that cites recorded evidence; two residents hold different beliefs about the same fact; a goal survives several activities and a night's sleep; and the chain from observation to outcome can be read with one query.

3

A village with stakes

Size M · Engine rules Built
Measuring

Give residents more to weigh. This stage is engine work and adds no model calls of its own. It adds situations in which a choice is real, which the pilot will have shown how to count.

  • Seasons and weather. A year of about 48 village days. Fields produce only in their season. Winter burns wood and eats the stores.
    The year is the real one, as decided on 22 November 2026 under open decision 10. Barley ripens only from April to October, by an amount set for each month, in good and poor years, and heavy rain at harvest lays some of it flat. Nothing grows from November to March. The founding barn holds 160 measures and each granary 120, and barley in store is slowly lost to mice and damp. Cold air sharpens appetites, and nobody comes up the road between November and February. engine/stores.mjs.
  • Households. Each cottage has its own larder and woodpile, so one household can run short while the village does not.
    Each cottage keeps its own woodpile and larder, and its people carry firewood and loaves home from the common stores by choice. A cottage hearth burns only from its own pile, so one household can wake cold while the village woodpile stands, and children eat from the larder. The hall keeps its lodgers’ fire from the common store. A broken bed, sweeping and rotten floorboards are as before. engine/households.mjs.
  • Lives. Courtship, marriage, children, old age and death. Illness from hunger, cold or overwork. A resident who is not housed or fed for long leaves.
    Residents have ages. Days of hunger, cold or overwork bring illness, which mends when they are fed, warm and rested, and a resident too ill to work is offered no work. Close, trusting pairs with a spark between them walk out together. Asking to marry, and the answer, are the residents’ own choices, and the wedding is on the green on a Sunday. A married couple keep one house, children are born into it, and at fourteen a child becomes a resident. A lodger moves into a cottage that stands empty. A resident ill too long, or unfed or unhoused for long, leaves. Death, by a long illness or old age, happens only where the village allows it: the Sandbox does, and the live village does not, as decided on 22 November 2026 under open decision 8. engine/lives.mjs.
  • A working day. Trades have hours, the mill and oven have queues, buildings decay and need the carpenter, and feast days are for the green.
    Trades keep hours. Nobody works at a trade on a Sunday or a feast day, and on a feast day the village eats together on the green. The mill and each oven take one worker at a time, and a second waits their turn. The common buildings wear, the carpenter’s repair work goes to the worst first, and a mill, oven or well in poor repair turns out less, or nothing once out of use. engine/workday.mjs.
  • Hunting and fishing. Residents can look for game in the coppice, gather bait and fish from a dry riverbank, and mend a worn fishing pole.
    They can dress game, tan hides, and cook, smoke or cure the catch. One catch of two fish can be cured and carried to the Storehouse. Raw catches and meat keep for 30 village hours. Processing uses the oldest stock first and a shift claims its ingredients. Cooked and preserved portions supply meals at the hall. Worn shoes make walking more tiring and can be re-soled with Storehouse leather. Oswin prefers hunting, and Hugh and Alice prefer fishing. Their live decisions weigh these preferences against their needs and responsibilities.
  • Outside events. A peddler, a tax collector, a storm, a stray herd.
    A peddler trades salt fish for linen on the green. The lord’s reeve takes a tenth of the barley and a length of linen at Michaelmas and Lady Day. Winter storms strip roofs, lay barley flat and bring down deadwood. Stray cattle eat a ripe field until someone who has seen them drives them out. engine/visitors.mjs.
  • A chronicle that selects. Each event carries a salience. Routine goes to a resident's own log. Measure food in days, not as a fraction of a bin, so that building a granary no longer reads as a shortage.
    Every event carries a salience from 0 to 10. The village snapshot serves the latest 100 notable entries.   • Keep internal food, tools and household provisions in communal stores, with no wages or personal accounts. A communal trading post can exchange actual surplus goods with visiting merchants.

  • Travelers may bring goods, stories and unfamiliar practices; residents learn them through witnessed encounters or reported accounts, not instant omniscience.

  2. A resident with no instruction and no reply. This plan has them wait indefinitely. The alternative is a default the engine applies after a long wait, which would be an action no model chose. That changes the agreed roles and needs an explicit decision.
  3. Who issues standing instructions. The action choice that is reused, the goal review that sets a routine, or both.
  4. How long a promise lasts in real viewing time. The starting assumption is a visitor who watches for two minutes and checks back tomorrow.
  5. What the audience may see before residents learn it. The starting assumption is a public record of observable events.
  6. How the action and goal budgets are divided, and whether the daily account in Stage 5 has a third.
  7. When the pilot reaches the live village. It is proved in a test village. Moving it to the live one changes what residents know about the stores. Note, 21 November 2026: oven observations and requests for help are already on main, which the live village deploys, so this has in effect been settled by deployment. It should be recorded as decided or reopened.
  8. Whether residents die, and whether the live village is migrated or a new one is founded when Stage 3 changes the economy.
  9. How far visitors may reach. Stage 9 can stop at any step. The courier trial in Stage 8 is the first to require moderation.
  10. How long a year is. Stage 3 proposes a year of about 48 village days. The calendar and the weather now keep the real year of 365, which at two village days to a real day takes six real months. One of the two has to give.
  11. Whether the wood store grows with the village. It holds 32 bundles at any size. With winter fuel that is about a day's burning for thirty hearths in frost, so a large village cannot lay in wood for a cold spell.
  12. What the common store's building is called. It was proposed as a commissary. “Storehouse” is the working name here, because the word fits 1312 and the plan already speaks of the common store. Decided, 22 November 2026: the Storehouse.
  13. Whether households keep a larder and a woodpile. Stage 3a keeps them as the place a day's allowance is carried to. A stricter commune would have everyone eat at a common table in the hall, and would undo part of Stage 3's households. Decided, 22 November 2026: households keep both, and the allowance is carried to the larder.
  14. Whether the oven's flour and firewood move to the storehouse. The promise pilot rests on Mabel counting them at the oven, and acceptance tests 4 and 9 name that count. Stage 3a leaves both where they are, and leaves household firewood at the woodpile. Decided, 22 November 2026: they stay at the oven.
  15. Who keeps the storehouse, and who sets the allowance. The keeper can be a second duty for a founding resident or the trade of the next arrival. The allowance can be an engine parameter or a bylaw the moot votes, which needs Stage 4. Decided, 22 November 2026: a new resident arrives to keep the storehouse as a trade, and the allowance is an engine parameter from the start. The moot can now vote to raise the adult allowance to 1.5 loaves.
  16. Whether the engine may leave a feasible option off the menu. Decided for Stage 3b, 22 November 2026. The fresh-choice menu aims for 12 with overflow for protected choices. The trace records omissions and the engine checks replies and standing instructions against feasibility.
  17. How many people a menu offers, and who. Decided for Stage 3b, 22 November 2026. Up to four named visits. Active relationships and responsibilities, proximity, warmth and time apart guide the shortlist.
  18. Whether company is offered by person or by place. Decided for Stage 3b, 22 November 2026. Casual company at public places and targeted visits to named people both have a place on the menu.
  19. How operators reach the admin panel. Choose whether to put it behind the existing private operator access or give it its own authenticated entry point. Both options need individually attributable changes and separate read and edit permissions.

§ 11 · NewFuture considerations

Questions the stages above do not answer. This section was added on 21 November 2026. Nothing here is scheduled or decided. Each is worth a deliberate answer before the point named under it.

  1. Keeping the two editions in step

    roadmap.md is the canonical plan and this page is a hand-made rendering of it. Both now carry the marks, so every change of status has to be made twice.

    Generating this page from the markdown would remove that.

    Beforethe next status changes.

  2. Proving a model change is safe

    Every exchange is stored with the exact request that was sent. Replaying stored requests against a candidate model or a reworded request would show how choices shift before the village feels it.

    Stages 1 and 2 both change what the request contains, and design decision 6 expects providers to change.

    Beforethe first change of model, or Stage 2's larger request.

  3. Records that only grow

    Events, the activity log and decision traces gain rows with every tick, and each resident's record grows with what they hold. Stage 6 wants an archive of every day.

    What is kept whole, what is summarised and what may be dropped needs deciding while the tables are still small.

    Note, 27 November 2026: a nightly trim is proposed under 7C, and the health report now shows each table’s size and growth per day.

    BeforeStage 6, or the first slow query.

  4. Many viewers at once

    The server reads and prepares each update once for all attached viewers. A connection that falls too far behind reconnects with a fresh snapshot. Measure bandwidth, memory and clock lateness with a hundred concurrent watchers.

    One snapshot built per tick and shared by everyone may be enough.

    BeforeStage 5's first public test.

  5. A village that can be read without the map

    Acceptance test 8 puts first-time viewers on phones. Both views are pictures: a visitor using a screen reader, or a small screen in the walking view, gets little of what is happening.

    A plain account of the current scene would serve them and would also feed Stage 5's director.

    Since then the director's caption has become a first plain account. It is text, it is announced to a screen reader, and it covers the one scene in view, not the village.

    Beforeacceptance test 8 is run.

  6. Model-written prose in public

    Stage 2 publishes a resident's own sentence, Stage 4 an account of what was said, and Stage 5 a daily account. Each is text nobody reviewed, shown under the village's name.

    The plan labels these as accounts. It does not yet say how they are screened, how a wrong one is withdrawn, or how they keep a period voice.

    BeforeStage 2's first published sentence.

  7. History beyond the parish

    Dalecrest was founded on 1 August 1312 and keeps the real Julian calendar. The Great Famine of 1315 to 1317 lies under three village years ahead, about a year and a half of real time at the present pace.

    Whether the outside world reaches the village at all is undecided, and a 48-day year would cut the calendar loose from history altogether.

    Withopen decisions 8 and 10.

  8. Knowing when the village is in trouble

    The caps bound what can be spent and the activity log records every wait. What tells an operator that the provider has been down for an hour, or that half the village has stood waiting since noon?

    The pilot's deliberate outage is the natural rehearsal, and open decision 2 depends on how long such waits really last.

    Beforethe pilot week.

  9. The tests that need a database

    Eight of the 59 tests require PostgreSQL. They include the ones that prove a restart yields each outcome exactly once, which is acceptance test 2.

    It is worth confirming that the deploy run includes them, so the promise is checked on every release and not only on a machine that happens to have a database.

    Beforeacceptance test 2 is signed off.

  10. Resident priorities in moot votes

    On 27 November 2026, replaying 32 live ballots in three option orders changed one granary vote from support to abstain. Mean support probability ranged from 78% to 92%, and all four motion outcomes held. The next question is whether voters have enough individual reasons to weigh a motion differently.

    Give each voter the goals, household obligations, commitments, and firsthand or relayed evidence relevant to this motion. Show what it will cost in labour and materials, who stands to benefit, what other work it would delay, and which claims are uncertain. A miller worried about grain spoilage and a lodger waiting for a cottage can weigh the same use of the building crew differently.

    Compare the existing and richer ballot requests offline with option order held fixed, then rotate the options as a separate check. Include shared benefits, competing projects, and cases where residents know different things. Judge votes by whether they follow the resident’s recorded priorities and evidence. Agreement is a valid result when those priorities align. Preserve the roll call and the evidence each voter received so a change can be traced to the record.
